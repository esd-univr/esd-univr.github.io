Enrico Fraccaroli is a postdoctoral researcher in ESD whose work connects **electronic design automation, cyber-physical systems and industrial digitalisation**. He received his PhD in Computer Science from the University of Verona with a thesis on a holistic approach to functional safety for networked cyber-physical systems, after earlier work on virtual-platform integration for smart-system simulation.

His research has addressed the modelling and simulation of heterogeneous systems across several abstraction levels. This includes manipulation and abstraction of analog and mixed-signal descriptions, transistor-level and HDL tooling, multi-domain fault models spanning electrical, mechanical and thermal behaviour, fault injection, network synthesis and simulation-based functional-safety analysis. A central goal is to make faults and cross-domain effects observable early enough that they can be studied before a system reaches physical deployment.

Enrico has also worked on wearable IoT for Parkinson's disease and on distributed embedded-system design. He was awarded a **Marie Skłodowska-Curie Global Fellowship** for STRATEGUS, carried out between the University of Verona and the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill. His current industrial research uses digital twins and deterministic simulation to validate and optimise cyber-physical production systems, bringing together models of machinery, communication and control software.
