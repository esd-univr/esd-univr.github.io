## Objective

**Bip-Bip** addressed **freezing of gait (FoG)**, a disabling motor symptom of Parkinson’s disease in which a person can become temporarily unable to initiate or continue walking. The project explored a wearable smart system capable of recognising conditions associated with an imminent freezing episode and providing timely assistance, with the broader aim of reducing the impact of FoG on mobility, autonomy and quality of life.

The official Veneto Region funding record identifies the project as **1695-23-11-2018**, submitted by the University of Verona under the POR FSE 2014–2020 programme *La ricerca a sostegno della trasformazione aziendale — Innovatori in azienda — Assegni di ricerca 2018*.

## Wearable sensing and prediction

The research line combined on-body sensing with lightweight machine-learning methods suitable for resource-constrained devices. Work by the Verona team during this period studied tri-axial accelerometer signals collected from the back, hip and ankle and the classification of gait into normal, freezing and **pre-freezing** conditions. The important shift was from detecting a FoG episode after it had started to recognising a precursor state early enough for an assistive device to react.

A peer-reviewed study from the team reported a k-nearest-neighbour classifier over features extracted from accelerometer windows and demonstrated the feasibility of pre-FoG recognition on constrained hardware. That result describes the technical research context in which Bip-Bip was developed; the site does not claim a publication-to-project link unless it is explicitly established in the curated bibliography.
