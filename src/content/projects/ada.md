## Objective

**ADA** investigated how digital technology could support ageing people with Down syndrome in maintaining autonomy, social well-being and healthy daily routines. The project was motivated by the longer life expectancy of people with Down syndrome and by the specific challenges associated with an earlier and accelerated ageing process.

Rather than treating coaching as a generic reminder system, ADA aimed to strengthen self-efficacy and help users cope with everyday activities through a personalised virtual-coaching platform adapted to the person's needs and coaching plan.

## Technical approach

The platform combined **unobtrusive smart objects**, Internet of Things technologies and data analytics. Smart objects provided observations from daily activities; analytics were intended to recognise the person's needs, match them against the coaching plan and help select an appropriate personalised response.

The project therefore brought together embedded and IoT systems with data analysis and a human-centred coaching model. The University of Verona project record describes the work as explicitly multidisciplinary, involving social-care professionals, coaching specialists, researchers in education for people with intellectual disabilities and ICT researchers working on IoT, embedded systems and data analytics.

## Demonstrator

Medication-taking was selected as a concrete use case for requirements gathering and feasibility assessment. It provided a daily-life activity in which the system could explore how smart objects, contextual information and personalised coaching could work together while keeping the broader goal focused on autonomy rather than on the technology itself.
